Vision

From high-quality proteins to function-controlling molecules — and to new medicines

Research on a drug target is only as good as the protein in hand. Our technical strength is the production of high-quality preparations of GPCRs and other membrane proteins, and on this foundation we build two pillars: structural analysis and the discovery of molecules that control the target.

Membrane proteins such as GPCRs occupy multiple conformational states depending on ligand binding and interactions with signaling partners. We capture these states by cryo-EM, combine them with pharmacological functional analysis to understand which structural state gives rise to which function, and then generate antibodies, monobodies/nanobodies, AI-designed de novo binders and DEL-derived small molecules that selectively modulate the desired state or function.

Our goal is to develop these molecules into diverse modalities that control protein function, and to carry them forward into new medicines.

Protein Production

Producing high-quality protein preparations

Neither structural analysis nor screening can begin without protein that is homogeneous, stable and active. For each target we build an expression system — baculovirus-infected insect cells or mammalian cells — and carry the membrane protein through solubilization and purification to the formation of complexes with G proteins, antibodies and other partners.

Every preparation is checked for homogeneity (monodispersity), stability and ligand-binding activity before it is used for structure determination or binder screening. We believe this accumulated quality control is what makes high-resolution structures and reliable screening results possible.

Developed across many membrane proteins, GPCRs above all, this production technology also serves our collaborations, where we take on the preparation of target proteins.

Keywords: baculovirus–insect cell and mammalian expression / membrane-protein solubilization and purification / complex formation / quality control (monodispersity, stability, ligand-binding activity)

Structure & Mechanism

From structure to molecular mechanism

Understanding a drug target requires knowing which conformations it adopts and how it interacts with ligands and signaling proteins. Using single-particle cryo-EM as a central platform, we determine high-resolution structures of GPCRs, other membrane proteins, and protein complexes.

We are particularly interested in forms of selectivity that are directly relevant to drug discovery: how different ligands bound to the same receptor preferentially engage G-protein versus β-arrestin signaling, how ligands distinguish among receptor subtypes within the same family, and how receptors select specific G-protein subtypes such as Gi or Gs.

Our studies have addressed biased signaling in KOR, ligand subtype selectivity in OX2R and the M2 muscarinic receptor, and Gi/Gs selectivity in EP3. By integrating structural analysis with pharmacology, molecular dynamics simulations, and spectroscopy, we aim to connect molecular recognition to intracellular signaling as a continuous mechanistic process.

Keywords: cryo-EM / ligand recognition / biased signaling / subtype selectivity / G-protein selectivity / pharmacology

Molecular Discovery

Creating molecules that control the target

With high-quality preparations in hand, molecular discovery moves much faster. We generate molecules that selectively control the state and function of a target protein in several formats: antibodies, monobodies/nanobodies, AI-designed de novo binders and small molecules from DNA-encoded library (DEL) screening.

Antibodies

Antibodies

We raise and screen antibodies in-house to obtain those that recognize a specific receptor conformation and control its function, as our allosteric EP4 antibody does. They serve as Fabs for structural analysis and as functional antibodies that modulate signaling.

Monobodies & nanobodies

Monobodies / nanobodies

Through collaboration we obtain small binders such as monobodies and nanobodies, which we use as tools to support structure determination and as modalities that act inside or outside the cell.

De novo binders

AI-designed de novo binders

Starting from structural information, we design protein binders to the target surface by AI-enabled de novo design, then express and evaluate them in-house.

Small molecules

Small molecules (DEL screening)

We run DNA-encoded library screens in-house against our high-quality membrane-protein preparations to discover small-molecule ligands.

Each molecule is then validated by complex structure determination and functional analysis to establish its mechanism of action, and developed as a functional modality that controls protein function, with new medicines as the goal.

Keywords: antibody production / monobodies and nanobodies (collaboration) / AI-enabled de novo binder design / small molecules by DNA-encoded library (DEL) screening / functional modalities

Current Directions

From structure to function-modulating modalities

We are extending our structural and functional platforms toward the development of molecular modalities that selectively modulate target-protein states and signaling outputs. Rather than treating structure determination as the endpoint, we iterate between protein production, structural analysis, functional evaluation, and molecular discovery to connect Protein Production → Structure → Molecular Discovery → Drug Development.

Our current work spans multiple modalities — antibodies, monobodies/nanobodies, AI-enabled de novo binders and DEL-derived small molecules — together with structure-based drug discovery (SBDD) and computational approaches to establish new principles and modalities for modulating protein function, and to develop them into new medicines.

Multiscale physiology

From the Transformative Research Area “Multi-scale platform for untangling physiological complexity”

Within the MEXT/JSPS Transformative Research Areas (B) program, Multi-scale platform for untangling physiological complexity, we worked to connect ligand-induced receptor structural changes with signaling, cellular responses, and organism-level effects across multiple scales. As principal investigator of the A01 project on rapid structural analysis of transient protein complexes, we developed structural approaches to capture functional protein complexes formed in distinct signaling states.

As one of the early outputs from this research area, we reported a structural and functional study of ligand-dependent signaling selectivity in KOR. Major additional results from the project are currently being prepared as multiple manuscripts.

We are now extending these advances toward integrated drug-discovery research that links Structure → Function → Modality.

Functional modalities

From an allosteric EP4 antibody to functional modalities

For the prostaglandin E2 receptor EP4, we determined the structure of the receptor in complex with the inhibitory antibody Fab001 at 3.2 Å and showed that an antibody bound to the extracellular surface can allosterically regulate orthosteric ligand binding and signaling.

Building on this concept, we are developing functional modalities — antibodies, monobodies/nanobodies, AI-enabled de novo binders and DEL-derived small molecules — that selectively modulate defined receptor conformational states and signaling outputs.

Targets & Platform

Research targets and integrated platform

GPCRs form the core of our research platform, while we are expanding toward membrane proteins and protein complexes in which structural biology can reveal new principles for drug discovery. A defining feature of the Protein Science Group is that we treat protein production, structural analysis, functional evaluation, and molecular discovery as one continuous research process — and that it starts with the technology to produce high-quality preparations.

Research targets

GPCRs at the core, expanding toward broader drug targets

Our major GPCR targets include opioid, orexin, prostaglandin E2, muscarinic, and bombesin receptors. We investigate ligand recognition, receptor activation, signaling selectivity, and subtype selectivity from both structural and functional perspectives.

Beyond GPCRs, we are expanding toward membrane proteins and protein complexes for which structural information can reveal new mechanisms of regulation and new opportunities for drug discovery.

Integrated platform

Connecting production, structure, function, and molecular discovery

On the foundation of high-quality membrane-protein production, our platform integrates single-particle cryo-EM, GPCR signaling assays, antibody production, AI-enabled de novo binder design, DEL screening, structure-based drug discovery, and molecular dynamics simulations. Monobodies and nanobodies are obtained through collaboration.

Rather than using these technologies in isolation, we cycle through Production → Structure → Function → Molecular Discovery to analyze drug targets from multiple angles and generate modalities that can be developed into medicines.

Targets: opioid / orexin / prostaglandin E2 / muscarinic / bombesin receptors and broader drug targets. Platform: protein production (expression, purification, complex formation, QC) / cryo-EM / pharmacology / antibody production / AI-based de novo binder design / DEL screening / SBDD / MD; monobodies and nanobodies through collaboration.
